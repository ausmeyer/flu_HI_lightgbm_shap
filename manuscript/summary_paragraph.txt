Across both the original H3N2 dataset and the WIC-derived H3N2 dataset, the sequence-based LightGBM analyses recovered a coherent antigenic signal centered on canonical HA1 head positions, particularly 144, 145, 158, 159, 189, and 193, while also identifying additional sites emphasized in the WIC study such as 53, 160, and 173. In the original H3N2 analysis, adding patristic distance produced little to no improvement in the primary site-state model and slightly weakened the clarity of the canonical site ranking, supporting the original non-patristic model as the main scientifically interpretable result. In the WIC analyses, the strongest intervention was not patristic adjustment but exclusion of egg-, mixed-, and unknown-passage measurements, which substantially improved predictive performance and reduced assay-related noise. The full WIC model best demonstrated continuity with both classical antigenic-site analyses and the original WIC Bayesian analysis, recovering substantial overlap with Neher/Bedford, cluster-transition, Koel, and WIC-reported positions. The filtered WIC model provided the cleanest interpretable sequence-based result, whereas the filtered-plus-patristic variant gave the best purely predictive performance but at the cost of a slightly weaker site-level literature correspondence. Overall, the results suggest that residue-level sequence models can simultaneously corroborate historically validated antigenic sites and the broader WIC site set, while providing a more directly predictive and mechanistically interpretable ranking of candidate antigenic determinants.
