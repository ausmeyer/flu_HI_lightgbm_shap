The paper should now be framed as a biological synthesis about antigenically informative H3N2 HA sites, not as a machine-learning benchmarking exercise. The main text should make one integrated argument across `Table 1` and three figures. First, the Neher/Bedford H3N2 analysis serves as the benchmark test of the approach: direct residue-level sequence models recover the canonical H3 antigenic-site signal emphasized by Koel, Neher/Bedford, and related cluster-transition analyses, and they do so without meaningful gain from adding patristic distance. Second, the Harvey/WIC analysis shows that the most important step toward a modern, interpretable large-scale model is passage filtering rather than phylogenetic adjustment: excluding egg-passaged, mixed-passaged, and unknown-passage observations yields a filtered Harvey/WIC site-state model that remains predictive while producing the clearest biologically interpretable site ranking. Third, the cross-study concordance analysis demonstrates the broader value of the work: the Neher/Bedford model most sharply recovers the canonical benchmark signal, whereas the filtered Harvey/WIC model provides the strongest overall agreement across otherwise weakly concordant literatures, including Harvey/WIC, Koel, Neher/Bedford, McWhite/passaging, PLoS/Meyer, and independent sequence-only LightGBM+SHAP analyses based on large unpassaged sequence datasets. Across these comparisons, SHAP-based sequence rankings perform substantially better than sitewise dN/dS as a practical guide to likely antigenically important positions. The supplement should reinforce this main claim by documenting primary-model diagnostics first and then showing that unfiltered Harvey/WIC and patristic analyses are informative sensitivity tests but do not alter the main biological conclusion.
