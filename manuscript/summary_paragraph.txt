Sequence-based machine-learning analyses of the Neher/Bedford H3N2 and Harvey/WIC H3N2 hemagglutination inhibition datasets recovered a stable antigenic signal centered on canonical HA1 head positions while also capturing broader site sets emphasized in more recent studies. In the Neher/Bedford H3N2 dataset, the primary sequence models strongly recovered the classical antigenic core, including substantial overlap with Koel and Neher/Bedford sites, with little benefit from adding patristic distance. In the Harvey/WIC dataset, the clearest scientific improvement came not from phylogenetic adjustment but from excluding egg-, mixed-, and unknown-passage measurements, yielding a filtered Harvey/WIC model that retained the canonical core while also aligning strongly with the Harvey Bayesian analysis. Cross-study comparison showed that this filtered Harvey/WIC site-state model had the strongest overall concordance with independent sequence-only LightGBM analyses trained on passage and sampling-date targets, including large strictly controlled unpassaged datasets, suggesting that it captures a robust and reproducible sequence-level antigenic signal rather than dataset-specific noise. Across studies, SHAP-based sequence rankings consistently outperformed sitewise dN/dS as a guide to biologically important antigenic positions. Together, these results support a manuscript centered on two primary models, the Neher/Bedford H3N2 model and the filtered Harvey/WIC H3N2 model, with broader multi-study comparison used to show convergence across classical antigenic literature, the Harvey/WIC Bayesian framework, passaging analyses, and independent sequence-only machine-learning approaches.
