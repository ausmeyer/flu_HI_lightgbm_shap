Sequence-based machine-learning analyses of historical H3N2 hemagglutination inhibition data recovered a stable antigenic signal centered on canonical HA1 head positions while also capturing broader site sets emphasized in more recent studies. In the original H3N2 dataset, the primary sequence models strongly recovered the classical antigenic core, including substantial overlap with Koel and Neher/Bedford sites, with little benefit from adding patristic distance. In the WIC dataset, the clearest scientific improvement came not from phylogenetic adjustment but from excluding egg-, mixed-, and unknown-passage measurements, yielding a filtered WIC model that retained the canonical core while also aligning strongly with the original WIC/Harvey Bayesian analysis. Cross-study comparison showed that this filtered WIC site-state model had the strongest overall concordance with independent sequence-only LightGBM analyses trained on passage and sampling-date targets, including large strictly controlled unpassaged datasets, suggesting that it captures a robust and reproducible sequence-level antigenic signal rather than dataset-specific noise. Across studies, SHAP-based sequence rankings consistently outperformed sitewise dN/dS as a guide to biologically important antigenic positions. Together, these results support a manuscript centered on two primary models, the original H3N2 model and the filtered WIC model, with broader multi-study comparison used to show convergence across classical antigenic literature, the WIC Bayesian framework, passaging analyses, and independent sequence-only machine-learning approaches.
