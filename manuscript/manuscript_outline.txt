PNAS manuscript outline

Article framing
- Journal target:
  `PNAS`
- Article type:
  `pnasresearcharticle`
- Main-text length target:
  about 10 to 12 pages, using most of the available space but staying disciplined
- Core identity of the paper:
  a biological synthesis and comparative inference paper about antigenically informative H3N2 HA sites, not a general machine-learning benchmarking paper

Working title
Sequence-based machine learning identifies robust determinants of H3N2 antigenic variation across hemagglutination inhibition datasets

Central claim
- Direct sequence-based residue-level models can recover a robust set of H3N2 antigenically informative HA sites from HI data.
- The strongest modern consensus signal emerges not from phylogenetic adjustment, but from careful control of passage heterogeneity in the Harvey/WIC dataset.
- The resulting rankings reconcile multiple literatures that have often been treated separately:
  Koel,
  Neher/Bedford,
  Harvey/WIC,
  McWhite/passaging,
  PLoS/Meyer structural-evolution work,
  and independent sequence-only LightGBM+SHAP analyses.

Why this paper matters
- Vaccine strain selection and antigenic surveillance depend on knowing which substitutions are likely to matter biologically.
- There are many ways to rank influenza HA sites, but they do not agree strongly.
- This paper identifies which modeling choices recover the most credible antigenic signal and which do not.
- The main take is practical:
  direct sequence-based models are useful,
  passage filtering matters materially,
  SHAP-style residue-level rankings outperform sitewise dN/dS as a prioritization tool,
  and the filtered Harvey/WIC model yields the strongest modern consensus signal.

Core narrative logic
- The Neher/Bedford H3N2 analysis validates the approach against the best-established H3 benchmark signal.
- The filtered Harvey/WIC H3N2 analysis shows that the same framework remains useful in a larger, more heterogeneous modern dataset once passage-related noise is reduced.
- Cross-study synthesis shows that these are not isolated results but part of a reproducible consensus across multiple independent analytical traditions.

Main-text structure

1. Significance statement
- Keep this broad and high-level.
- Avoid method-centric language unless it serves the biological claim.
- Main points to convey:
  sequence-based models can directly prioritize antigenically informative H3 HA sites;
  passage filtering clarifies the modern WIC signal;
  the recovered sites converge across multiple influential literatures;
  this has direct relevance for surveillance and vaccine interpretation.

2. Abstract
- One paragraph, about 220 to 250 words.
- Recommended structure:
  problem -> gap -> approach -> two primary results -> cross-study synthesis -> implication
- Essential content:
  mention Neher/Bedford H3N2 and filtered Harvey/WIC H3N2 as the two primary analyses;
  note that patristic distance adds little compared with passage filtering;
  note that SHAP-based rankings outperform dN/dS for this application;
  close with surveillance/vaccine relevance.

3. Opening introduction text
- Paragraph 1:
  motivate antigenic drift as the biological problem and explain why sequence interpretation matters for vaccine updates and immunity forecasting
- Paragraph 2:
  explain why HI data remain central but analytically difficult, especially when different studies emphasize different site sets and model structures
- Paragraph 3:
  review the main prior frameworks:
  Koel and related cluster-transition work,
  Neher/Bedford antigenic phenotype prediction,
  Harvey/WIC Bayesian mapping,
  McWhite passaging analyses,
  and sequence-only ML approaches
- Paragraph 4:
  state the gap clearly:
  there is still no unified, residue-level, directly predictive comparison that tells us which H3 HA sites are most robustly supported across these disparate approaches
- Final sentence of introduction:
  explicitly state that the goal is to identify robust antigenically informative HA sites and to determine which modeling choices best recover a credible consensus signal

4. Results

Results section overview
- Use `Table 1` first to orient the reader to the three non-patristic analyses.
- Then move quickly into the three main result blocks anchored by the three main figures.
- Keep the model grid in the background; the emphasis should stay on biological interpretation.

Results subsection 1
Direct sequence models recover the canonical H3 benchmark signal in the Neher/Bedford dataset
- Introduce the Neher/Bedford dataset briefly and concretely.
- Clarify that this analysis functions as the strongest benchmark test because it is closest to the established H3 cluster-transition literature.
- Use `Table 1` to summarize site-state and substitution performance.
- Anchor the subsection on `Figure 1`:
  Neher/Bedford H3N2 site-component top-30 plot.
- Main points to make:
  the ranked sites recover the dominant benchmark head-region positions;
  the site-state model provides an interpretable decomposition;
  the substitution model is also strong but does not need to lead the narrative;
  patristic distance does not materially improve the biological interpretation.
- End the subsection with a conclusion sentence:
  the method passes the benchmark test.

Results subsection 2
Passage filtering yields the clearest modern antigenic-site model in Harvey/WIC
- Introduce Harvey/WIC as the larger and more heterogeneous test case.
- Explain briefly why passage matters biologically and analytically.
- Use `Table 1` to show that the filtered analysis remains strong predictively.
- Anchor the subsection on `Figure 2`:
  filtered Harvey/WIC H3N2 site-component top-30 plot.
- Main points to make:
  the filtered model retains the core canonical benchmark positions;
  it also recovers additional Harvey/WIC-associated sites;
  filtering clarifies the signal far more than patristic adjustment does.
- This subsection should make the most important conceptual claim of the paper:
  better biological control of the data is more valuable than adding a coarse phylogenetic-distance covariate.

Results subsection 3
Cross-study synthesis identifies a robust consensus set of antigenically informative H3 sites
- Anchor the subsection on `Figure 3`:
  cross-study concordance.
- Explain that this figure is the paper’s synthesis step, not just a comparison exercise.
- Compare the two primary models against:
  Koel,
  Neher/Bedford,
  Harvey/WIC,
  McWhite unpassaged dN/dS,
  PLoS/Meyer,
  and the independent LightGBM+SHAP sequence-only rankings.
- Main conclusions to make explicitly:
  the Neher/Bedford model most sharply recovers the benchmark Koel/Neher signal;
  the filtered Harvey/WIC model has the strongest broad agreement across otherwise weakly concordant literatures;
  SHAP-based sequence rankings outperform dN/dS as a practical prioritization tool for likely antigenically important sites.
- This subsection should make the case that the paper’s value lies in synthesis and adjudication across frameworks.

Results subsection 4
Sensitivity analyses support the primary interpretation
- Keep this subsection short in the main text.
- Main points only:
  unfiltered Harvey/WIC is harder to interpret and shows the cost of passage heterogeneity;
  patristic distance changes background prediction more than mechanistic attribution;
  the main conclusions are robust to alternative encodings and sensitivity analyses.
- Send most details to the supplement.

5. Discussion

Discussion paragraph structure
- Paragraph 1:
  restate the core biological result:
  direct sequence-based models can recover robust antigenically informative H3 sites
- Paragraph 2:
  interpret why passage filtering matters more than patristic adjustment
- Paragraph 3:
  explain why this matters scientifically:
  the goal is not just to identify sites under selection, but to prioritize sites that are informative for antigenic phenotype and therefore relevant to vaccine mismatch and immune escape
- Paragraph 4:
  explain the two complementary model roles:
  Neher/Bedford H3N2 as the strongest benchmark reconstruction,
  filtered Harvey/WIC H3N2 as the strongest modern consensus model
- Paragraph 5:
  discuss why SHAP-based rankings outperform dN/dS for this application
- Paragraph 6:
  limitations
  include dependence on available HI data, passage handling, sequence matching, and the fact that the study prioritizes sites rather than experimentally proving mechanism
- Final paragraph:
  close on surveillance value and prospective use in interpreting emergent H3 substitutions

9. What to avoid
- Do not frame the paper as:
  “we tried many machine-learning models”
- Do not let patristic analyses dominate the narrative.
- Do not let the full model grid substitute for a clear biological claim.
- Do not turn the cross-study synthesis into a catalog of every prior site list.
- Do not oversell Bayesian versus ML as a methodological contest; keep the focus on which analyses best identify biologically useful antigenic-site signals.
