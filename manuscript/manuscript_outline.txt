  concise Research Report format, ideally near 6 to 8 pages and clearly below the 12-page maximum

  title, author block, significance statement, abstract, keywords
- Preferred section order for this paper:
  opening introduction text without an explicit `Introduction` heading, then `Results`, `Discussion`, `Materials and Methods`, `Acknowledgments`, `References`

Working title
Sequence-based machine learning identifies robust determinants of H3N2 antigenic variation across Neher/Bedford and Harvey/WIC hemagglutination inhibition datasets

Core PNAS message
- Antigenic drift prediction matters for vaccine update decisions.
- Direct residue-level sequence models recover the classical H3 antigenic core.
- Passage filtering in WIC improves interpretability more than adding patristic distance.
- A filtered Harvey/WIC site-state model shows strong agreement with both classical antigenic literature and large independent sequence-only models.

Main-text narrative
- Primary model 1:
  `Neher/Bedford H3N2`
- Primary model 2:
  filtered `Harvey/WIC H3N2`
- Everything else:
  supporting evidence for robustness, interpretability, and convergence across studies

1. Significance statement
- 50 to 120 words.
- Audience:
  broad biology readership, not just influenza specialists.
- Main points to hit:
  sequence-based models can identify antigenically important H3 sites directly from HI data;
  filtered Harvey/WIC analysis reduces passage noise;
  key signals converge with classical antigenic literature and independent large-scale sequence-only analyses;
  the framework is relevant to antigenic surveillance and vaccine strain evaluation.

2. Abstract
- Maximum 250 words, one paragraph.
- Structure:
  background -> question -> approach -> main results -> interpretation.
- Must emphasize only the two central models:
  `Neher/Bedford H3N2` and filtered `Harvey/WIC H3N2`.
- Mention supporting comparison to prior work in one sentence only.

3. Opening introduction text
- Problem:
  antigenic drift complicates vaccine strain selection and population immunity forecasting.
- Empirical substrate:
  HI assays link sequence change to antigenic phenotype.
- Prior frameworks:
  Koel, Neher/Bedford, Harvey/WIC, McWhite passaging work, and sequence-only ML approaches.
- Gap:
  no unified, directly predictive, residue-level comparison across classical and modern H3N2 datasets.
- End with explicit study aim:
  identify robust antigenically relevant HA sites using interpretable sequence-based models and compare them across datasets and prior analytic frameworks.

4. Results

Results subsection 1
Sequence-based models recover the classical H3N2 antigenic core
- Introduce the Neher/Bedford H3N2 dataset and modeling setup briefly.
- Show predictive performance of additive, site-state, and substitution models.
- Emphasize that the Neher/Bedford H3N2 analysis sharply recovers canonical antigenic sites and classical literature overlap.
- Briefly note that patristic distance adds little and does not improve the main mechanistic interpretation.

Results subsection 2
Passage filtering yields the clearest interpretable model in WIC
- Introduce the Harvey/WIC dataset and explain passage heterogeneity briefly.
- Show that filtering egg, mixed, and unknown passage rows improves clarity and performance.
- Present the filtered Harvey/WIC site-state model as the preferred contemporary model.
- Emphasize overlap with both classical sites and Harvey/WIC positions.

Results subsection 3
Cross-study comparison identifies a robust consensus antigenic signal
- Compare the two primary models to:
  Koel, Neher/Bedford, Harvey/WIC, McWhite Virus Evolution, PLoS/Meyer, and the review-manuscript LightGBM passage/date/entropy signals.
- Main take:
  `Neher/Bedford H3N2` best recovers the sharp classical canonical signal;
  filtered `Harvey/WIC H3N2` provides the best overall cross-study consensus.
- Explicitly note that SHAP-based sequence rankings outperform dN/dS as a prioritization tool for likely antigenically important sites.

5. Discussion
- Interpret why passage filtering matters more than patristic adjustment.
- Interpret why direct sequence models are more useful for practical antigenic-risk questions than broader latent inclusion summaries alone.
- Explain the split between two notions of success:
  sharp classical recovery versus broad cross-study consensus.
- State preferred models clearly:
  `Neher/Bedford H3N2` for canonical antigenic reconstruction;
  filtered `Harvey/WIC H3N2` for consensus modern prioritization.
- End on broader relevance:
  sequence-based antigenic site prioritization can support surveillance, candidate-strain interpretation, and mechanistic follow-up.
