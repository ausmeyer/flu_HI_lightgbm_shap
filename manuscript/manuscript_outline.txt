Title
Sequence-based machine learning recovers canonical and contemporary determinants of H3N2 antigenic variation across historical HI datasets

Core framing
- Main model 1: original `H3N2`
- Main model 2: filtered `H3N2-WIC` excluding egg, mixed, and unknown passage rows
- Supporting analyses:
  substitution encodings, patristic-distance variants, full WIC, and cross-study comparison to Koel, Neher, Harvey, McWhite, PLoS/Meyer, and the review-manuscript LightGBM models

Main claim
- Direct sequence-based models recover the classical H3N2 antigenic core.
- Passage filtering in WIC improves interpretability more than adding patristic distance.
- The filtered WIC site-state model shows the strongest overall cross-study concordance with independent sequence-only models and prior antigenic literature.

1. Introduction
- Antigenic drift is central to vaccine update decisions, mismatch risk, and population immunity.
- HI data provide one of the main empirical links between HA sequence change and antigenic phenotype.
- Classical work identified recurrent antigenically important H3 sites:
  Koel sites, Neher/Bedford cluster-transition sites, and related historical substitution analyses.
- Recent work from Harvey/WIC used a broader Bayesian framework on a larger H3N2 HI dataset.
- Remaining gap:
  a directly predictive, residue-level sequence analysis that can be compared across datasets and across prior methodological frameworks.

2. Study aims
- Build reproducible sequence-based modeling pipelines for the original H3N2 and WIC H3N2 HI datasets.
- Estimate which HA sites repeatedly improve prediction of HI phenotype.
- Distinguish the effects of passage filtering from the effects of adding phylogenetic-distance covariates.
- Place the resulting site rankings in the context of prior antigenic, evolutionary, and machine-learning studies.

3. Data and preprocessing
- Original H3N2 dataset:
  sequence matching, alignment, mature HA numbering, homologous-titer standardization.
- WIC dataset:
  finalized virus/serum inputs, HA1 extraction, assay-context annotation, homologous standardization, additive context correction.
- Numbering convention:
  all reported sites use mature HA protein numbering with the 16-aa signal peptide removed.
- WIC passage categories and filtered analysis:
  rationale for excluding egg, mixed, and unknown passage rows from the preferred WIC model.

4. Modeling framework
- Additive context baseline and context-corrected titers.
- Site-state model:
  virus residue state, serum residue state, and site-change indicator.
- Substitution model:
  explicit serum-to-virus substitution identity features.
- Cross-validation:
  grouped holdouts and out-of-fold prediction summaries.
- Interpretation:
  SHAP-based site importance and fold-stability summaries.

5. Primary result I: original H3N2 model
- Report predictive performance of additive, binary, site-state, and substitution models.
- Emphasize strong recovery of canonical antigenic positions and classical literature overlap.
- Show that patristic distance adds little predictive value and weakens mechanistic clarity.
- Main interpretation:
  the original H3N2 model is the clearest reconstruction of the classical antigenic core.

7. Cross-study comparison
- Use the integrated comparison table to compare the two primary models to:
  Koel, Neher/Bedford, Harvey/WIC, McWhite Virus Evolution, PLoS/Meyer, and the review-manuscript LightGBM site metrics.
- Key result:
  `H3N2` most sharply recovers the classical canonical antigenic literature.
- Key result:
  filtered `H3N2-WIC` gives the best overall consensus with classical sites plus the independent sequence-only passage/date LightGBM signals.
- Key result:
  SHAP-based sequence rankings outperform dN/dS as a guide to antigenically important positions.

8. Supporting analyses
- Full WIC model:
  used to show continuity with the original Harvey/WIC Bayesian analysis and to demonstrate the effect of passage filtering.
- Substitution models:
  useful for classical-site recovery and mutation-specific interpretability, but secondary to the site-state framing.
- Patristic variants:
  modest effect on prediction, little or negative benefit for mechanistic site attribution.
- Optional note on two-cluster WIC behavior:
  likely passage-context related rather than explained by divergence alone.

9. Discussion
- Why direct predictive sequence models are useful for antigenic-risk inference.
- Why passage filtering matters more than patristic adjustment for WIC interpretability.
- Why patristic distance is best treated as a sensitivity covariate rather than a primary control.
- Why Bayesian inclusion, SHAP importance, dN/dS, entropy, and passage/date prediction are related but not identical questions.
- Strongest synthesis:
  sites that recur across these diverse frameworks are the most credible candidates for biologically meaningful antigenic importance.

10. Limitations
- HI datasets remain heterogeneous and assay-dependent.
- Original H3N2 and WIC datasets differ in design, coverage, and historical scope.
- SHAP importance is not causal proof.
- WIC filtered analyses trade sample size for cleaner assay/passaging control.
- Patristic-distance sensitivities depend on approximate phylogeny construction.

11. Conclusions
- Sequence-based models robustly recover known H3 antigenic determinants.
- The original H3N2 model best recovers the classical canonical antigenic signal.
- The filtered WIC site-state model provides the strongest cross-study consensus ranking.
- Cross-study agreement is strongest for SHAP-based sequence models and weaker for dN/dS-only summaries.
