Title
Sequence-based machine learning recovers classical and contemporary determinants of H3N2 antigenic variation from historical HI datasets

Core claim
Direct sequence-based LightGBM models trained on standardized HI measurements recover a stable canonical antigenic core, substantially overlap both classical H3N2 antigenic-site literature and the WIC Bayesian analysis, and show that passage filtering improves interpretability more than adding phylogenetic-distance covariates.

1. Introduction
- Motivate antigenic drift as a central problem for vaccine strain selection and mismatch assessment.
- Frame HI data as one of the main empirical links between viral sequence change and antigenic phenotype.
- Introduce classical H3N2 antigenic-site work: Koel sites, Neher/Bedford cluster-transition perspective, repeated mutation framework.
- Introduce the WIC dataset and its Bayesian site-mapping study as a richer modern resource.
- State the gap: a need for directly predictive, sequence-level models that can be compared across datasets and against prior biological knowledge.

2. Study goals
- Build a unified, publication-ready modeling workflow for the original H3N2 and WIC H3N2 HI datasets.
- Compare predictive and interpretive results across site-state and substitution encodings.
- Test sensitivity to phylogenetic-distance covariates.
- Test sensitivity to passage filtering in WIC.

3. Data and preprocessing
- Original H3N2 dataset: sequence matching, alignment, mature-protein numbering, homologous-titer standardization.
- WIC dataset: finalized sequence/HI inputs, HA1 extraction, assay-context annotation, homologous standardization.
- Explain mature HA numbering and HA1 restriction for WIC.
- Explain passage categories and rationale for excluding egg, mixed, and unknown passage measurements in sensitivity analyses.

4. Modeling framework
- Additive baseline correction.
- Site-state model:
  virus amino-acid state, serum amino-acid state, and site-change indicator.
- Substitution model:
  explicit serum-to-virus substitution identity features.
- Cross-validation design:
  grouped virus holdouts and Neher-style held-out comparisons.
- SHAP-based site ranking and fold-stability summaries.

5. Sensitivity analyses
- H3N2 base versus H3N2 plus patristic distance.
- WIC base versus WIC plus patristic distance.
- WIC full versus WIC filtered passage data.
- WIC filtered versus WIC filtered plus patristic distance.

6. Main results: original H3N2
- Report predictive performance of additive, binary, site-state, and substitution models.
- Show strong recovery of canonical antigenic positions and Koel sites.
- Show that patristic distance adds little to the main site-state model and slightly weakens interpretability.
- Recommended figure set:
  predicted-vs-actual, CV comparison, site-state site bar, site-state stacked component plot, site stability, substitution site bar.

7. Main results: WIC
- Report performance for full WIC data.
- Show substantial overlap with both classical sites and WIC-reported sites.
- Emphasize that the full WIC model bridges classical and contemporary site sets.
- Recommended figure set:
  predicted-vs-actual, CV comparison, site-state site bar, site-state stacked component plot, site stability, substitution site bar.

8. Main results: WIC filtered-passage analysis
- Report performance improvement after excluding egg, mixed, and unknown passage rows.
- Show the filtered model yields the cleanest interpretable sequence result.
- Compare top sites with the full WIC model and discuss which sites persist.
- Contrast filtered non-patristic and filtered-plus-patristic models:
  prediction improves slightly with patristic distance, but literature correspondence weakens.

9. Comparison to prior work
- Compare overlap with Neher/Bedford cluster-transition sites.
- Compare overlap with Koel sites.
- Compare overlap with the WIC Bayesian site set.
- Main interpretation:
  the ML framework corroborates both the classical antigenic core and the broader WIC site set, but yields a more directly predictive ranking of recurrent antigenic determinants.

10. Discussion
- Why passage filtering matters more than phylogenetic adjustment for interpretability.
- Why patristic distance is best treated as a sensitivity covariate rather than a primary control.
- Why direct predictive sequence models are useful for vaccine mismatch and antigenic risk assessment.
- Clarify that posterior inclusion and predictive importance answer related but distinct questions.

11. Limitations
- HI data remain assay-dependent and historically heterogeneous.
- WIC and original H3N2 are not perfectly matched in design or coverage.
- SHAP ranks are still model-based importance summaries, not causal proof.
- Tree-based patristic distances depend on approximate phylogeny and branch-length estimation.

12. Conclusions
- Sequence-based models robustly recover known antigenic determinants.
- WIC data expand but do not replace the classical antigenic core.
- Filtered non-patristic WIC is the preferred interpretable model.
- Patristic variants are useful sensitivity checks, not primary mechanistic models.
